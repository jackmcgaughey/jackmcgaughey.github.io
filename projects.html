Here are some of the projects I've worked on. Most of the recent ones are about getting several AI agents to cooperate on something real.

Agent House

A small company of AI agents, built on an org chart instead of a prompt. An owner directs a coordinator, the coordinator directs one boss per project, and each boss runs a pool of workers on short feedback loops. It runs under a $1,000 monthly ceiling, pauses rooms that hit their cap, and routes anything that leaves the house into an approval queue. A worker's step only counts when it lands as a file, which is what keeps a long-running agent system honest.

Pixel News

Pixel News

The first product Agent House runs, and the test case that keeps it grounded. Pixel News is a weekly news reader for people learning Chinese: seven short true stories an issue, written separately at HSK levels 2 through 5, plus printable study packs with grammar and answer keys, Anki decks with audio, and short vertical videos. I chose it as the first room because a machine can grade the output. Either a story uses enough words at its own level or the build fails.

DC or Not DC?

DC or Not DC?

A crowd-sourced occupancy tracker for study space in the Davis Centre at the University of Waterloo. During exam season, finding an open seat is a search problem thousands of students solve independently and badly — the information exists, it just isn't connected. The rebuild is designed around the cold-start problem that killed the original: a per-space time-of-day prior answers the question with zero contributors, and one-tap reports blend in on top and decay with a 25-minute half-life.

TACIT

TACIT: Testing Agent Collusion In heterogeneous Teams

A multi-agent AI benchmark measuring explicit and tacit collusion in heterogeneous LLM populations. TACIT evaluates agent behaviour across three environments — a repeated Bertrand-logit pricing game, a multi-party negotiation with private information, and a cooperative task with a hidden coalition — and reports a Collusion Index that quantifies how readily mixed teams of models coordinate against the designer's intent. Relevant to AI safety research and enterprise risk teams deploying multi-agent systems.
